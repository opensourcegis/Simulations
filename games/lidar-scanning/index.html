How airborne LiDAR paints the ground

A LiDAR sensor fires a laser and times the returning pulse to measure range. A single beam only sees one spot — so every scanner needs a beam-steering mechanism that sweeps that spot across the flight path while the aircraft carries it forward. The mechanism decides the ground pattern of the point cloud. These are the mechanisms in the market:

MechanismOptic & ground patternRepresentative systems
Oscillating mirror
(galvanometer)		Flat mirror swings; sinusoidal zig-zag, points bunch at the swath edges.Leica ALS40–ALS80, Optech ALTM
Rotating polygon mirrorBeam strikes each spinning facet in turn; straight parallel one-way lines.RIEGL VQ / VUX, Optech
Palmer / conical
(nutating mirror)		Tilted mirror spins about the axis; overlapping ellipses, fore & aft looks.TopoSys, conical topo scanners
Risley prisms
(dual rotating wedges)		Light refracts through two counter-rotating wedges; non-repetitive rosette.Livox Avia, DJI Zenmuse L1 / L2
MEMS micro-mirrorGimbaled micro-mirror tilts on two axes; Lissajous tile in a narrow FOV.Innoviz, automotive-class UAV units
Rotating multi-beam
(spinning head)		Laser modules ride a spinning head; concentric rings, one per channel.Velodyne / Ouster / Hesai, YellowScan
The Scanner optics panel shows the beam hitting the real moving surface — the facet, the mirror face, or the prism — and the footprint it traces. On the map you can scroll to zoom and drag to pan to inspect individual laser points and their spacing.
