Mission briefing

You are the pilot. Plan a photogrammetry survey that actually flies.

Every control starts at zero or at the bottom of its range. Nothing here is set for you — the plan on the screen right now is a legal flight that would waste a battery and deliver nothing. Build it up yourself.

  1. 1
    Pick a takeoff site Tap a pad on the map. The trees around each pad show the clearing you actually get: a wide opening means room to abort and somewhere to put down, a tight one means neither. Sitting further from the block also costs you the transit.
  2. 2
    Point the headings at the wind Drag the two compass dials. Take off into the wind for lift and control. Fly your survey legs across the wind so the long passes stay straight.
  3. 3
    Fly the altitude the brief asks for The survey is commissioned at 2.5–3.0 cm/px — where that puts your height is yours to work out. Two hard rules bound you: never plan above the 120 m ceiling, and whatever you overfly needs clear air beneath you. Plan into a mast and you will watch the aircraft go in.
  4. 4
    Find the overlap that works Side and forward overlap both start at zero and nobody will tell you where to put them. Too little tears the model apart; too much stretches the route until the battery cannot carry it home. The review board's verdict is your only feedback.
  5. 5
    Mind the ground and the weather, then commit The airframe is rated for 50 minutes and roughly 2 km² per battery in calm, 20 °C air — heat, cold and wind all tax it before takeoff, and every hill under the route is a climb the pack pays for. In rolling country, launch from the lowest ground you can. Land with 25% in hand.

The five terms that matter

GSD — ground sample distance
How much ground one pixel covers. 3 cm/px means each pixel is 3 cm across. It is set almost entirely by how high you fly — lower is sharper, higher is coarser.
Side overlap
How much neighbouring strips overlap side-to-side. Higher overlap packs lanes closer together, so the flight gets longer and you cross the hills more often.
Forward overlap
How much consecutive photos along a lane overlap. Higher overlap means photos taken closer together — more frames to carry home and stitch.
Usable endurance
The airframe is rated 50 minutes in calm, 20 °C air. Wind and temperature tax it before you leave the ground — the conditions tile shows what today actually gives you.
Terrain climb
The vertical the route flies because the ground underneath it rises and falls. It never shows up in the route length, but it comes straight out of the battery.
