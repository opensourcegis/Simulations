Mission briefing

You are the pilot. Plan a photogrammetry survey that actually flies.

  1. 1
    Pick a takeoff site Tap a pad on the map. Sites differ in how clear they are and how far the drone must transit to reach the block — both cost you.
  2. 2
    Point the headings at the wind Drag the two compass dials. Take off into the wind for lift and control. Fly your survey legs across the wind so the long passes stay straight.
  3. 3
    Balance the camera against the clock Lower altitude means sharper images but narrower strips, so more lanes and a longer flight. More overlap means safer processing but more photos and a faster trigger.
  4. 4
    Clear every check, then submit The camera cannot trigger faster than once per second, and you must land with battery to spare. Slow down or climb if you are outside the limits.

The four terms that matter

GSD — ground sample distance
How much ground one pixel covers. 1.2 cm/px means each pixel is 1.2 cm across. It is set almost entirely by altitude: fly lower, get a smaller number, see more detail.
Side overlap
How much neighbouring strips overlap side-to-side. Higher overlap packs lanes closer together, so the flight gets longer.
Forward overlap
How much consecutive photos along a lane overlap. Higher overlap means photos are taken closer together, so the camera has to fire faster.
Trigger interval
Seconds between shots. Real cameras need about a second to write a frame. Below that, you miss photos and the survey has holes.
